## How to sign

Signing is a one-time step per GitHub account.

On your first pull request, the **CLA Assistant** bot will leave a comment asking you to sign. Reply on that PR with:

> I have read the CLA Document and I hereby sign the CLA

Your signature is recorded. Subsequent PRs from the same GitHub account skip the prompt automatically.

If you ever need to re-sign (e.g. the CLA text materially changes), comment `recheck` on the PR and the bot will refresh the check.

## Who needs to sign

External contributors. The repository maintainer ([@HBarefoot](https://github.com/HBarefoot)) and automated bots (e.g. Dependabot) are exempt via the workflow's allowlist.

## Why we have a CLA

Engram is, and will remain, MIT-licensed open source (see [`BUSINESS_MODEL.md`](BUSINESS_MODEL.md)). The CLA exists for two reasons:

1. **Clean copyright assignment.** If contributors retain copyright with no license grant, the project can't be relicensed later — even to another OSS license — without contacting every past contributor. The CLA grants the relicensing right up front so the project keeps long-term flexibility.
2. **Acquirer-clean codebase.** If Engram is ever adopted by a larger entity (see the *Acquisitions* section of [`BUSINESS_MODEL.md`](BUSINESS_MODEL.md)), the acquirer needs a clean chain of IP rights. The CLA pre-empts the diligence pain that otherwise stalls these conversations.

**The CLA is permissive — it does not prevent you from using your own contribution however you like.** You still own your work. You're granting a license to Engram, not transferring copyright.
